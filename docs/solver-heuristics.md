# The solver's heuristics: what each protects, and what breaks without it

*The ledger #187 asked for. Measured on `main` at b92d400 (Windows, release), by `scripts/solver-bar/ablate.py`. It replaces section 4 of [solver-audit.md](solver-audit.md), which predates the audit's PR 3, PR 5 and #489.*

Every rule and constant layered onto the solve (`newton` in `crates/drift-solver/src/lib.rs`) and the drag (`crates/drift-edit/src/editor/drag.rs`) is listed here with what it protects, the tests that guard it, and what the sketch corpus does without it.

## How it was measured

Each heuristic has a switch (`drift_solver::ablation::Switch`). The switches exist only in builds with the test-only `ablate` feature; in every other build each is `false` at compile time, and the code is as it was. With a switch thrown (`DRIFT_ABLATE=<name>`), its rule is off, or its constant changed as the row says. For each switch in turn:

- **Guarding tests**: the normal tests of drift-solver and drift-edit were run, and the ones that fail are listed. A heuristic no test fails without has no guard.
- **Corpus**: the sketch corpus (the solver bar's item 8: 9,510 drags of 51 sketches, every frame judged) was swept and compared, drag by drag, with the same build with nothing thrown. "Newly fail" counts drags that fail a check they passed, by check: `R` rows met, `S` arc sweep flip, `W` way-round flip, `L` line turned end for end, `G` held radius grew, `J` jump over 50 times the pointer, `X` refused frame moved geometry, `C` the release, `P` out and back, `O` outside the part. "End elsewhere" counts drags whose part ended anywhere else, to the last bit. "Solves" is the sweep's drag solves against the base's.

**Verdict.** *Breaks*: a test fails or a corpus drag newly fails a check. *Nothing breaks; drags end elsewhere*: no test and no check fails, but results change, so the rule does something nothing here measures as better or worse. ***Nothing breaks without it***: no test fails, no check fails, and every drag ends where it did, to the bit: on this evidence the rule is dead weight, or guards a case neither the tests nor the corpus holds (the row says which is likelier).

What the verdicts can't see: the corpus sits at the origin at one size (the similarity census, item 2 of the bar, is what measures size and place), it has no Wall-tool walls, and its drags are a pointer's (typed moves, nudges and relation adding are other paths). A rule that only acts there reads as dead here. **No heuristic should be deleted on this table alone**: a row's flag is where to look, and the deletion is a PR judged by the whole bar.

To run it again (hours for every switch; `--only` for a few):

```
python scripts/solver-bar/ablate.py --tests --out DIR [--only strides,fold-try]
```

This page is written by `scripts/solver-bar/ledger.py` from `ledger_rows.py` (the columns written by hand: what each heuristic is and protects) and `ablation.json` (the run's numbers). Change a row there, not here.

### Nothing breaks without it: why, row by row

- `edit-preference-bound`: an edit's rule (PR 6), and the sweep's only edits are its releases, which begin with their shapes met. Its case (a line kept square stretched 40 km) has no test: **it needs one before anyone touches it**.
- `leave-storage`: it only acts where the floor of ulps is above the bar, about a million part-sizes from the origin (#377's seeded census, 100 km out). The corpus and the normal tests are at the origin: the similarity and seeded censuses are its guard, not these.
- `settle-meets-relations`: its case is one census drag (7930). No test pins it and no corpus drag reaches it: **the likeliest real candidate for a pinned test, or for deletion once the census agrees**.
- `lowered-line-flip`: walls only (relations lowered onto a wall's centreline). No corpus sketch has a Wall-tool wall, and `face_dimensions_hold_through_drags`' light run doesn't reach it: the heavy matrix's wall property is its guard.
